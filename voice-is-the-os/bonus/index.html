Question one

“What is ASR?”

ASR is automatic speech recognition: the part of a voice agent that turns what you say into text. It's the agent's ears. ElevenLabs sells its own, called Scribe, and launched Scribe v3 that same day.

So we'd heard plenty about Scribe. As far as I heard, nobody on stage said “ASR” all day. The first thing standing between a room of AI-curious people and a free piece of merch was a vocabulary test in industry jargon.

People froze, guessed, asked it to repeat itself, and tried again. Eventually the staff standing nearby started helping people through it. The humans were in the loop the whole time. They just weren't part of the design.

The agent needed ASR to hear you say “ASR.”

Question two was easier, so easy I can't remember it. Question three asked which company has the best voice models. And that was it. Three spoken questions, plus the retries, multiplied by every person in the line, added up to well over an hour.

The other line of thought

I've seen this booth before. It was a bank's phone line.

Strip out the merch and the booth is a pattern I see in regulated industries all the time: a voice agent placed in front of something a customer wants, gated by questions that serve the business more than the customer.

At the boothQuestion one asked for jargon I hadn't heard on stage all day.
On a bank's phone lineSecurity questions customers can't answer, like the exact amount of a recent deposit. They fail, call back or give up.
At the boothStaff stood nearby and helped people get past the question.
On a bank's phone lineReps coaching callers through verification. If people routinely need help to pass a check, the check isn't working.
At the boothQuestion three had one obvious answer: the host.
On a bank's phone lineSurveys built to flatter. You get a score, not a signal.
At the boothThree self-serve booths and no one at a table.
On a bank's phone lineA contact center scored on containment. The dashboard says “automated.” The customer says “an hour.”

What I'd tell anyone putting a voice agent at the front door

  1. Ask only what the moment needs.The booth needed to know which item you wanted. Everything after that felt like a quiz.
  2. Speak your customer's language, not your roadmap's.“ASR” is how builders talk. Customers say “it didn't hear me.”
  3. Design the human in.The staff ended up in the loop anyway. Make the hand-off official, quick and logged, so it's a feature instead of a workaround.
  4. Measure the line, not the bot.Every second a voice flow adds gets multiplied by everyone waiting behind it. Containment rate won't show you that. Wait time will.
  5. Friction should protect someone.Banks add friction on purpose: fraud checks, step-up verification, cooling-off periods. Good friction protects a customer. This friction protected free merch.

The kicker

The tech worked. The questions didn't.

The summit was incredibly well run, which is exactly why this line stuck with me. The voice agent did its job. Someone just decided to put a quiz between people and the thing they came for, and to ask the hardest question first.

Question three asked which company has the best voice models. Maybe the one asking does. But the best model in the world can't rescue a question nobody should have been asked.
